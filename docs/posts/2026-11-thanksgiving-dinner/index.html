Thanksgiving Dinner, Measured in Hours of Work

Prices spiked in 2022. Paychecks caught up. Last year, dinner took the least work in 40 years.

Every November, the American Farm Bureau Federation shops for the same classic Thanksgiving dinner for 10: a 16-pound turkey, stuffing, sweet potatoes, rolls, peas, cranberries, a veggie tray, pumpkin pie with whipped cream, and milk and coffee. They’ve done it every year since 1986.

The dollar price has roughly doubled since then. But that’s only half the story. So, as usual, we asked: how long does the average worker have to work to buy it?

The short answer

In 2025, the classic dinner for 10 cost $55.18. At the average worker’s pay, that’s 1.7 hours of work, or about 10 minutes per guest. That’s the least work it has taken in the survey’s history. In 1986, it took 3.2 hours.

40 years of Thanksgiving dinner

Hover to see the price each year. The dollar cost peaked at $64.05 in 2022, when inflation and bird flu sent turkey prices soaring. That year dinner took 2.3 hours of work, a visible bump. Since then, the price has fallen three years in a row while pay kept rising, so dinner got easier to afford on both counts.

Dollars vs. work

2025 $55.18 $31.79 1.7 10

In dollars, Thanksgiving is about twice as expensive as in 1986. In work time, it’s about half the work.

What about this year?

The Farm Bureau releases its 2026 survey the week before Thanksgiving. We’ll update this post when it comes out. With food prices rising again this fall, we’ll be watching to see whether the streak of cheaper dinners holds.

The fine print

  • One menu, national average. The survey prices the same classic menu every year; your actual dinner, store, and region will differ. Regional prices in the Northeast and West run well above the national average.
  • A few early years are estimates. The year-by-year series we used fills a few early gaps using inflation data; the Farm Bureau’s own figures anchor the start and recent years.
  • Pay is the average worker’s hourly earnings in November, before taxes, for the roughly 80% of private-sector workers who aren’t managers.

The bottom line

Thanksgiving dinner feels more expensive because the receipt is bigger. But measured in the time it takes to earn it, last year’s feast was the most affordable in four decades: about 10 minutes of work per guest.

How we did this. Dinner costs are the American Farm Bureau Federation’s annual Thanksgiving Dinner Cost Survey (classic menu for 10). Years 1987–2022 come from a compilation by 24/7 Wall St.; 1986 and 2023–2025 are from AFBF. Pay is average hourly earnings of production and nonsupervisory employees for November of each year, via FRED. AFBF survey
