Americans Are More Generous Than You Think

A record $617 billion given to charity, 75 million volunteers, and more than half of us helping a neighbor. A little Thanksgiving good news.

We spend a lot of time on this site digging into what life costs. This week, for Thanksgiving, we wanted to look at the other side of the ledger: what Americans give. The numbers are a nice reminder that, whatever the headlines say, an awful lot of people are quietly showing up for each other.

The short answer

Americans gave a record $617.2 billion to charity in 2025, about $1.7 billion every day. 75.7 million people volunteered through an organization, giving 4.99 billion hours of their time. And 137.5 million, more than half of us, helped a neighbor.

We give a lot of money

In 2025, total U.S. charitable giving crossed $600 billion for the first time, reaching $617.2 billion, according to Giving USA 2026. That was up 5.7% from the year before.

The biggest share didn’t come from foundations or corporations. It came from regular individuals: about $394.2 billion, roughly two-thirds of the total. That’s checks to churches, donations to food banks, gifts to schools and charities, and countless $20 contributions that add up.

We give a lot of time

Money is only part of it. In the latest national survey by the Census Bureau and AmeriCorps:

Source: U.S. Census Bureau and AmeriCorps, Civic Engagement and Volunteering Supplement.
What Americans did (Sept 2022 – Sept 2023) Number
Formally volunteered through an organization 75.7 million people (28.3% of those 16+)
Hours volunteered 4.99 billion
Economic value of that time about $167.2 billion
Informally helped a neighbor (errands, house-sitting, lending tools) 137.5 million people (54.2%)

To put 4.99 billion hours in perspective: that’s the same as about 2.4 million people working full time for an entire year, for free.

And volunteering is bouncing back

The pandemic knocked volunteering down hard. Between 2021 and 2023, it jumped from 23.2% to 28.3%, the biggest increase since the government started tracking it in 2002. It’s still a little below where it was before COVID, but it’s heading the right way. Some of the biggest gains came from Millennials and from people with lower incomes.

The biggest giving day of the year is coming

Giving Tuesday, the Tuesday after Thanksgiving, has become a giving holiday of its own. In 2025, about 38.1 million Americans gave a total of $4 billion in a single day, a record.

This year it’s Tuesday, December 1. If you want to join in, it doesn’t have to be money: a few hours at a local food bank, shelter, or school counts too. So does checking on a neighbor.

The fine print

  • Giving totals are estimates from Giving USA, published by the Giving USA Foundation with Indiana University’s Lilly Family School of Philanthropy. They include giving by individuals, foundations, bequests, and corporations.
  • Volunteering figures are from 2023, the latest national survey; new data is expected in 2026.
  • The “2019” volunteering rate is calculated from the Census Bureau’s statement that the 2023 rate was 1.7 points below pre-pandemic levels.
  • One honest note: the number of people who give to charity has been slipping for several years, even as total dollars rise. More money is coming from fewer, larger donors.

The bottom line

It’s easy to feel like everyone is only looking out for themselves. The data says otherwise: hundreds of billions of dollars, billions of hours, and tens of millions of small kindnesses between neighbors, every single year. That seems like something worth being thankful for.
