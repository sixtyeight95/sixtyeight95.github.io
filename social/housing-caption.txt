"Nobody can afford a house anymore." Is that true? 🏠

We measured mortgages in HOURS OF WORK instead of dollars. A full-time month is about 173 hours. Here's how many of them the mortgage on a typical new home eats:

📉 2021: 56 hours
📈 2023: 87 hours
🏠 Today: 71 hours
😮 1990: 96 hours
🔥 1981: 124 hours

So the monthly payment isn't the worst it's ever been. The real wall is the down payment: 10% down now equals 7+ months of full pay, vs 5.6 months in 1985.

And interest rates are the big lever. At 2021's rates, today's typical new home would cost about $740 less per month.

Are you renting and saving, or did you lock in a low rate? 👇
